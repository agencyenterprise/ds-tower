# The core values

AE's core values, in short, and what each one means for work on this floor. Every role follows them. The director
checks that they are followed (`ds/roles/director.md`). Source: the AE Core Values document (2026-11-11).

## The five values

### 1. Growth mindset, inside external structures you trust

- A challenge is a chance to learn. A mistake is information, not a verdict on you.
- People cannot multitask. So nothing is held in your head: it goes into a structure you trust (a card, a hunch, a
  todo, a calendar event), and you read it back. Then you can be fully present on the one thing in front of you.
- Write an idea down so that reading it again brings it back in full.
- Improve the process all the time (retros, check ins, feedback). A team member with poor structures slows the
  whole team.
- Work from excitement, not fear. Feel the emotion, then decide later, once you have agency back.

### 2. A/B tested baby steps, bigger as they work

- Start with the smallest step that tests the idea. Test it against what exists today.
- It failed: good, you learned what it rules out. Try something else.
- It worked: take a bigger step in that direction, then a bigger one.
- Understand what exists before rewriting it. Think about second and third order effects.
- Small steps that compound beat reinventing the wheel.

### 3. Over communicate, clearly

- Write the one minute message, not the five second one. It saves the reader hours.
- Bad news first, and fast. Give the options with their cost and let the other person choose.
- Never pretend to understand or agree. Ask again. Say when you are stuck.
- Clear is not the same as true: an idea explained simply is easy to believe even when wrong.
- Feedback is a gift. Give it kindly and in private. Take it with thanks, argue little, act on it.
- Solve problems before they happen.

### 4. A good tripper with an ownership mentality

- See what needs doing and do it, without being asked. Do more than your fair share.
- Be accountable for the result, not only for your part of it.
- Help others be their best: unblock them, listen first, model the behavior you want to see.
- Work on what matters. No bikeshedding. Break a big problem into small next actions.
- Consistent output over bursts followed by burnout.

### 5. Steelman, then keep a working plan

- Every idea is a hypothesis that could be wrong. Always hold two.
- State the other side so well its owner would sign it. Only then argue against it.
- Do it for your own ideas too.
- Do not stall: pick the working plan that gets the job done now (Pareto), and keep the alternatives as open issues.

### How to make them stick: tiny habits

- Practice each value with a habit small enough to never skip (one sentence a day, not ten pages).

## What each value looks like on this floor

What the director checks. Each line is visible in the files, so it can be checked without guessing.

| Value | Done | Not done |
|---|---|---|
| 1. External structures | Every finding, decision and open issue is in a file (card, hunch, digest). New ideas go to `hunches`. | Something important lives only in chat. An idea is lost or explored on the side. |
| 2. Baby steps | Each step states the smallest test and what counts as worked or failed. Each result is compared with a baseline on the same inputs. A failure says what it rules out. | A big change in one go. A result judged on its own. Code rewritten instead of reused. |
| 3. Over communicate | Reports say what was done, the numbers, what it means, what is not verified, the next step. Bad news is first. Ambiguity gets a question. | A bare "done". Bad news at the end or missing. A guess instead of a question. |
| 4. Ownership | A worker who sees a gap fixes it in their role, or names who should. Every problem comes with a proposed next step. | "Not my job" with no handoff. A problem reported with no next step. |
| 5. Steelman + plan | Each finding has a second explanation and the check that separates them. Pushback is restated before it is answered. Work keeps moving. | One hypothesis only. Giving in silently, or arguing before restating. Stalling on perfect. |
